What I Do

What goes into an online store that sells

Every store is different, but these four parts are in almost every project.

Store build

A new store on Shopify or WooCommerce, or a rebuild of one that has outgrown its theme: catalogue structure, collections, product templates and a checkout that is easy to finish.

Payments and shipping

Payment providers, shipping zones and rates, taxes, and the emails customers get after they order, set up and tested with real test orders before launch.

Integrations

Connecting the store to the tools the business already uses, such as accounting, stock, subscriptions or a CRM. I connected one coffee store to QuickBooks so the store and the accounting talk to each other.

SEO and tracking

Clean collection and product pages for search, structured data, a fast theme, and GA4 ecommerce events so you can see revenue by product and by channel.

Shopify or WooCommerce

Shopify or WooCommerce: which one fits your store

Shopify is usually the simpler choice: hosting, security and updates are handled for you, and it runs well with a small team. WooCommerce runs on WordPress, so it suits stores that are content-heavy or need deep customisation, but it needs more maintenance. I build on both, and I will recommend the one that fits, even when it is the smaller job. For Shopify-specific work, see my Shopify developer page.

I also take on WooCommerce web developer work on existing stores, and B2B ecommerce website development where customers need their own prices, bulk ordering or account-based buying.

What Goes Into a Store

What an ecommerce website build includes

A store is more than a theme and some products. Before launch I set up the parts that decide whether it sells: a product structure that makes sense for your catalogue, collection pages people can filter, a checkout with the payment methods your customers expect, shipping rules and taxes, transactional emails, and the legal pages. Then the parts that let it grow: clean URLs and product data for search, GA4 ecommerce events, and the Meta pixel if you plan to advertise.

FAQ

Questions about building an online store

How do I build an online store?

Pick a platform (usually Shopify or WooCommerce), set up products and collections, connect payments and shipping, write clear product pages, and test the checkout with real orders before launch. Then set up tracking and SEO so people can find the store and you can see what sells. I can do all of it, or the parts you would rather not.

What is an ecommerce website?

An ecommerce website is a site where customers can browse products, pay and place orders online, with stock, payments, shipping and order emails handled by the platform behind it.

How much does an ecommerce website cost?

It depends on the platform, the number of products, the design and the integrations. After a free call I send a written fixed quote, so you know the cost before anything starts. Platform fees, such as a Shopify plan, are paid to the platform separately.

Can you take over an existing store?

Yes. A lot of my store work is on existing stores: speed, broken features, a theme upgrade or an integration that never worked properly.

Do you work with UK and US brands?

Yes, online. Portugal shares the UK time zone, and my afternoons overlap with the US East Coast morning.

How long does it take to build an online store?

It depends mostly on the number of products, how ready the content is and how custom the design needs to be. I give you a timeline with the written quote, and I tell you early if anything on your side, like product photos, is likely to hold it up.

Planning an online store, or fixing one?

Tell me what you sell and where you sell it. I will tell you honestly which platform and setup I would use.
